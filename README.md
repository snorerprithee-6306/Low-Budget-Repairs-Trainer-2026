## 🔥 Project Overview

**This tool** is a comprehensive trainer for **Low-Budget Repairs**. This trainer enhances the renovation experience by modifying money, tool durability, inventory resources, mission progress, and movement-related quality-of-life options. It is designed around the game's repair jobs, equipment purchases, object placement, apartment customization, and side activities.

**Target Audience:** Players who want to focus on the game's renovation jobs, story activities, exploration, and apartment customization without grinding for money, replacing worn tools, or repeatedly collecting materials.

> **⚠️ IMPORTANT:** This tool is for **educational and private use only**. It does not modify any game files — all changes are temporary and reset when the game closes. The tool is **undetectable**, but **use it at your own risk**. Always back up your save files before use.

## 📖 Detailed Feature Guide

### Movement & Exploration

Move through apartments, streets, shops, and job locations using the standard game controls. Activate movement enhancements only after entering gameplay. Disable them before cutscenes, scripted interactions, or loading screens to reduce the chance of physics and collision problems.

### Tools & Inventory

Use the inventory to manage tools, construction materials, furniture, and other job items. Unlimited materials and inventory protection are intended for building, carrying, assembling, and storing objects. Avoid changing item values while the inventory window is open unless the trainer specifically supports that action.

### World & Environment

Accept renovation and repair work, visit the hardware store and pawn shop, and complete objectives throughout the 1990s apartment setting. The game includes painting, plastering, tiling, electrical work, wood paneling, furniture assembly, window and door installation, cleaning, fire-related tasks, collectibles, and story activities. Money and material options are most useful before shopping or beginning a large job.

### Combat & Weapons

There is no traditional combat gameplay in Low-Budget Repairs. Progress is based on renovation work, object interactions, exploration, business decisions, and story missions rather than fighting.
